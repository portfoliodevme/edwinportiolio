Sentinel Awareness: Social Engineering Simulation and Training Platform

A Flask web application for running authorised, consent-based awareness campaigns. Participants practise identifying fictional suspicious messages, receive immediate educational feedback, and can continue learning through training modules and assessments.

Project overview

Sentinel Awareness gives organisations and educational institutions a controlled way to teach people how to recognise social engineering. Administrators create campaigns from fictional scenarios; participants interact with those simulations inside the application's own inbox rather than through unsolicited external messages.

The experience combines practice with education: after a participant follows a simulated link, the platform explains the warning signs and provides guidance. Training modules, assessments, campaign analytics, and downloadable reports help administrators plan follow-up learning.

Designed for learning, not credential collection

Simulated messages use fictional senders and lead to educational content. The platform is designed not to request or collect participants' real passwords or authentication codes.

Problem and project goals

One-off presentations and static guidance can be difficult to translate into practical habits, and they provide little evidence about which topics need reinforcement. At the same time, awareness exercises must protect participants and be run with clear authorisation.

This project brings safe simulations, consent controls, guided learning, and measurable programme outcomes into one application. Its goals are to:

  1. 1.Let administrators build and manage campaigns with fictional scenarios.
  2. 2.Give participants a controlled inbox and immediate learning feedback.
  3. 3.Manage informed consent and withdrawal throughout participation.
  4. 4.Support training modules and pre- and post-training assessments.
  5. 5.Measure campaign and learning outcomes with privacy-aware analytics.
  6. 6.Provide administrators with readable, exportable programme reports.

Platform features

Separate administrator and participant experiences support the full awareness-programme lifecycle.

Campaign and scenario management

Administrators create campaigns, set objectives and dates, select participants, and manage fictional social engineering scenarios. Campaigns move through draft, scheduled, active, paused, completed, and archived states.

Participant simulation inbox

Participants can review simulated messages, follow a training link, report a suspicious message, and see educational guidance tied to the scenario.

Learning and assessments

Published training modules and assessments support self-paced learning. Assessment attempts record scores, pass status, duration, and answers, including pre- and post-training attempts.

Administrator workspace

Role-specific dashboards bring campaign activity, participant status, training progress, assessment performance, analytics, and reporting tools together for programme administrators.

Application architecture

The application uses Flask's application-factory pattern. Routes are organised into blueprints, and reusable business logic is separated into service modules that work with SQLAlchemy models.

  |-- Participant interface

Flask application and role-specific blueprints
        |
Services: campaigns, participants, simulations, training,
          assessments, analytics, activity, and reports
        |
SQLAlchemy models and database
  |-- MySQL / MariaDB deployment
  |-- SQLite test configuration

The codebase separates authentication, campaigns, participants, simulations, training, assessments, analytics, reports, and administrative routes. Database migrations are managed with Flask-Migrate and Alembic.

Privacy, consent, and safe operation

Consent and privacy are central to the exercise. Participants make an informed choice, can withdraw without providing a reason, and are not penalised for opting out. Withdrawal blocks future assignments and revokes outstanding simulation tokens.

  • Simulations are delivered through the application's internal inbox; external email is disabled by default.
  • Fictional scenarios provide educational pages rather than real authentication forms.
  • Simulation tokens are stored as SHA-256 digests, expire, and can be revoked.
  • Participant reporting and campaign exports use pseudonymous codes rather than exposing unnecessary identity details.
  • Administrator and participant permissions are separated, with CSRF protection on state-changing forms.
  • Additional safeguards include password hashing, login attempt controls, rate limiting, and secure HTTP response headers.

Use only with authorisation and consent

The application is intended for approved awareness programmes. Its simulations should be configured and run transparently, with appropriate consent and institutional oversight.

Analytics and reporting

Campaign analytics summarise participant-level outcomes, including delivery, opens, simulated-link clicks, and reports. Scenario comparisons help identify where additional education may be useful. Repeated interactions are deduplicated so they do not artificially inflate event totals.

The wider programme view also brings together consent and participation status, assessment averages and pre/post comparisons, training completion, and participant activity. Rates are derived from recorded data with guards for empty denominators.

Administrators can generate printable reports and export campaign or institution-wide summaries to PDF and Excel. Exports are designed to use pseudonymous participant codes.

Typical user workflows

Administrator

  1. 1.Prepare scenarios, training content, and assessments.
  2. 2.Create a campaign and assign eligible, consenting participants.
  3. 3.Activate the campaign and monitor aggregate activity.
  4. 4.Review outcomes, plan follow-up learning, and export reports.

Participant

  1. 1.Register and review the participation consent information.
  2. 2.Review simulated messages in the application inbox.
  3. 3.Report or inspect a simulation and read the learning guidance.
  4. 4.Complete training and assessments, or withdraw consent.

Current scope and next steps

The current project focuses on internal inbox simulations, self-paced training, assessments, analytics, and downloadable reports. External email is not the default delivery method and requires explicit configuration and approval.

Potential future improvements include broader accessibility and mobile usability testing, richer training content, approved notification integrations, and additional scheduling and reporting options.
